Why This Exists

The gap between labor and ownership

Women make up ~70% of the coffee workforce — hand-harvesting, processing, fermenting — yet own just 20–30% of farms and hold far fewer leadership roles in cooperatives and trade.

Café Feminista is Joe Coffee's response: a sourcing program that centers women who identify with feminism as leaders, not just labor — amplifying their work, their voices, and their economic power.

Our Commitments

What we're holding ourselves to

Rhetoric without accountability is marketing. These are the specific, measurable commitments that govern how Café Feminista operates — and what we'll publish annually to prove it.

01
Transparent pricing
We will disclose the floor price and any gender premium paid for every Café Feminista lot — published on the product page.
02
Annual impact report
Each year we will publish the number of women-led farms sourced from, total dollars directed to women producers, and % of our single-origin menu they represent.
03
Producer-led storytelling
Every lot is attributed. Every page is written in conversation with the producer, not about them.
04
Third-party verification
We are working toward partnership with IWCA or Partnership for Gender Equity for external audit of our sourcing claims.

Founder, Musa Café · Córdoba, Veracruz, México
Blanca's family has farmed in Veracruz since 1960. Through Musa Café, she has transformed that inheritance — building a nursery that supplies healthy seedlings to neighboring farms, refining processing techniques, and forging her own direct path to international markets.

What's in the cup reflects who grew it

Bright and expressive, this washed lot carries notes of cream soda, almond, and Meyer lemon on a smooth, creamy body. It is balanced and lively — a coffee that shines as a pour-over or in milk.

That expressiveness is not accidental. It reflects Blanca's precision in processing: careful fermentation windows, meticulous drying, and the quality standards she applies to every plant she grows.

Every sip supports a woman building tangible economic power — not through charity, but through the market value her skill commands.
